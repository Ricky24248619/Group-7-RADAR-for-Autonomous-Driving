# Revised Risk Register

**CITS3200 Professional Computing | Group 07 | Revision 2 | 5 September 2026**

> Revises the Risk Register of 12 August. Original IDs are retained and never re-used,
> so R-01 here is the same risk as R-01 there. Each carries its **status at 5 September**
> with the evidence that changed it. New risks discovered during Sprint 2 are numbered
> from R-28.

## Method

Three rules from the original, unchanged. A risk is owned by one named person, not by
the team. A dependency failing is recorded as a failed dependency, not an issue inside
this project, because that changes how it is communicated to the client. Identifiers are
permanent and never re-used.

| Rating | Likelihood | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| High | Expected to occur unless acted on | Blocks a deliverable or forces a change of direction |
| Medium | Reasonably possible this semester | Delays a deliverable or forces significant rework |
| Low | Unlikely but credible | Absorbed within existing contingency |

**Status vocabulary:** Open · Reduced · **Closed** · **Materialised** (it happened) ·
**Disproven** (the premise was false).

## 6. What this register says overall

Two risks dominate and both sit outside the team's control.

**R-28 and R-09 are the same underlying dependency.** Client availability blocks
approval, and the Kaya paperwork blocks compute, and compute blocks the benchmarking
that the project exists to do. Work remains possible meanwhile: independent cold reads,
baseline/checkpoint selection, TruckDrive survey/record integration and protocol review.

**R-31 is the honest consequence.** There is no complete benchmark number. We can say
precisely what each dataset contains, that a model runs, and what a full run would cost
— but not how models perform. That gap is a resourcing outcome, and it is documented
rather than disguised.

## 7. Review cadence

Reviewed at the end of each internal sprint, with likelihood and impact reassessed.
New risks may be raised by any member at any time and are added with an owner before the
next meeting. High-impact risks are raised with the client rather than managed silently.
Failed dependencies are logged against the dependency.
